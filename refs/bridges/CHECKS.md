# CHECKS.md — bridge critic checklist

Apply to **one screenshot** of our bridge (chase view or side view). Answer each item
**yes / no**. Ordered most damning first: a `no` high on the list outweighs any number of
`yes` answers below it.

`L` = one painted lane width in the shot. Use it as the ruler; no world scale needed.
If the screenshot cannot answer an item (e.g. no pier is visible), mark it `n/a` and say so —
do not score it as a pass.

**Verdict rule:** any `no` in items 1–5 = the bridge fails, regardless of the rest.
Items 6–10 are scale and finish; 2 or more `no` there = fails.

1. Is the bridge exactly where the road centreline crosses the water, with the deck axis following the road (a curving road gets a curving deck)?
2. Is the deck grade continuous with the road on both sides — no kink, no step, no forced-level deck between sloping approaches?
3. Are the abutments and wingwalls buried in a graded embankment that runs continuously into the riverbank — no free-standing slab edge in mid-air, no cliff, no terrain poking through the wall?
4. Does every pier continue down into the riverbed with bed material closing around its base — no pier ending at the water plane, no footing or plinth sitting on the water surface?
5. Is there a hard dark shadow band under the deck fascia, and does the water surface stay clear of the deck and piers (no clipping, no hole in the river at the bridge)?
6. Is the bridge clear width less than or equal to the approach carriageway width — i.e. is the bridge the narrowest point on the road rather than wider than it?
7. Is the railing 0.29–0.40 L tall and visually thin (see-through steel rail, or a parapet no more than 0.20 L thick)?
8. Does the deck fascia depth read as span/14 to span/20 — between 0.55 and 1.6 times the railing height — so it is neither cardboard nor a slab?
9. Is the concrete or stone non-uniform (per-pour tone variation, horizontal lift lines or stone coursing, vertical drip streaks) with a visible dark waterline stain band where the structure meets the water?
10. Is there approach guardrail physically connected to the parapet ends, with road markings and camber continuing unbroken across the deck?
